# DevDen Blog Rules

This blog is for members of the Developer Den community to share things that are **interesting**, **true**, and **ours**. 
Posts are reviewed by a staff member before they are published.

> [!NOTE]
> We don't generally want to be too strict with this -- after all, the point of this is for fun! However, as with everything, some rules are needed.
> There's a lot here, most of which come down to common sense, but please make sure you've read them before contributing, for everyone's sake

## What to write about

Your own work, ideas and experiences, or things about the community. Some good examples of this are:
- A project you built, or a progress update on a project (whether yours or a community one like DevDenOS)
- Something you recently learned and/or how you learned it
- Something interesting or funny that happened in the community
- Your experiences or thoughts on a community event (e.g. DevDen Day)

Some things that are **not a good fit**:
- Copied or lightly reworded content, or generic news roundups that have nothing of your own in them
- Advertising or self-promotion with no real content

## Length

Posts don't need to be big or perfect! A few sentences about something you're working on or something you saw is a perfectly good post, so don't wait until you have something "worthy".

> [!TIP]
> Think of the blog as a longer version of a Discord message. If you'd post about it in the server, it's probably a good fit for a post. Just make it a _little_ longer than a few words :)

## Tone

- Be respectful and kind about the people and projects you write about.
- Strong opinions are fine when you back them up. Posts that only rant, or preach a "manifesto", will probably be declined.

> [!NOTE]
>  A post called "Why Language X Sucks" is fine if it makes fair, well-argued points. If it's mostly venting or the arguments don't hold up, it probably counts as a rant.

## People

Don't identify anyone who hasn't agreed to it.

- No real names, usernames, IDs, or any other identifying information
- No descriptions that makes someone easy to pick out
- This applies to people outside the community too
- If you have someone's permission, make this clear, providing evidence (over private channels) if relevant.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Talking about someone in a closed Discord server is one thing. Publishing about them on a public website is another.

## Your own work

- You must have written any posts yourself, or have permission to share it
- Don't include private or personal information (duh)
- Images must be yours, or you must have the right to share them (e.g. a licence that allows it). Add alt text and credit the source where relevant

## The AI Policy

In short, just don't use it -- we want to hear from _you_, not a robot. 

- Using AI for spelling and grammar checking is _acceptable_, but not encouraged. The posts should still be in your own voice, about things you actually did or know.
- Any low-effort or "slop" posts will be declined.

## License

By opening a pull request, you agree to publish your post under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). 
You keep your copyright, and anyone can reuse the post so long as they appropriately credit you.

## Who can post

Posting is open to any members of the Discord server over level 30 (this may be adjusted). Exceptions may be made for trusted members. 

## Review and Removal

- A maintainer / staff member will manually review every post. We may request changes or fully decline a post, and we'll give a reason why.
- Being asked to change something isn't usually a punishment, or a personal attack.
- We can unpublish a post that breaks these rules, and we will remove a post on request from its author or from someone it identifies. This may come with consequences such as removal of access.
- If you spot a problem with a published post, open an issue or message a maintainer.

## Authors

Each author has one profile file in `authors/`. Your profile is public. Only put information on it that you're happy to be shown.
